CV

Welcome! I'm a Ph.D. Candidate in Economics at U.C. Berkeley.

I am a macroeconomist with interests in Monetary Economics, Financial Economics, Expectation Formation, and Household Finance. I study how households, firms, and financial markets form expectations. My current projects examine the welfare costs of inflation, the role of information frictions and overconfidence in firms' expectations and aggregate inflation dynamics, the effects of FOMC disagreement on monetary policy uncertainty, and the influence of households' experiences of local house-price growth on mortgage borrowing and delinquency.
